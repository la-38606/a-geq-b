A ≥ B
Prover How it works

Prove a polynomial inequality

Type an inequality. A≥B searches for an explicit algebraic certificate — a way of rewriting the difference of the two sides that is visibly nonnegative — and verifies it with exact rational arithmetic before it claims a proof.

Polynomials over the rationals, with + - * / ^. Add assumptions with given, e.g. a^2 + b^2 >= 2 given a*b = 1.

Examples

Not finding a certificate can mean any of:

  • the claim is false;
  • the claim is true but has no sum-of-squares certificate (the Motzkin polynomial is the classic example);
  • a certificate exists but lies beyond the searches this prover implements.

A≥B never concludes “false” — it only ever reports that a verified proof was or was not found.
